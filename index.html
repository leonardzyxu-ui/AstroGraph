The observer location defines the zenith Z, nadir Z′, and a true horizon perpendicular to the zenith direction.
Latitude rule: the altitude of the visible celestial pole equals the magnitude of the local geographic latitude.

About AstroGraph

AstroGraph is a high-performance, mathematically accurate 3D celestial sphere explorer built to visualize the fundamental rules of positional astronomy.

Bespoke 3D Engine

Rather than relying on a large 3D framework, this model uses a custom canvas projection pipeline. It computes vector relationships, perspective depth, and front-versus-back arc drawing directly on an HTML5 canvas, which keeps the interaction responsive on typical laptops and tablets.

Astronomical Accuracy

  • Bright-Star Anchors: The background uses a curated set of real bright-star coordinates, plus a faint ambient star field, so the sphere has recognizable celestial reference points without turning into a full planetarium.
  • Latitude Rule: The core relationship shows that the visible celestial pole rises above the horizon by an angle equal to the magnitude of the observer's latitude.

Inertial Physics & Easing

  • Tactile Momentum: Swiping the sphere transfers your mouse velocity into mathematical inertia, using a friction coefficient to glide to a stop.
  • Sidereal Drift: When motion is enabled and the model sits idle for a few seconds, the star field and celestial equator begin a gentle drift around the polar axis while the local horizon frame stays fixed.
